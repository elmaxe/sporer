# A Spore-style creature editor

What Spore's creature creator does, what the game already had, and how the prototype editor (`creature.html`) puts the two together. Code: `src/gen/creature.ts` (the design and how it grows), `src/gen/creatureMotion.ts` (gait and IK), `src/creaturelab/` (the page). Tests: `tests/creature.test.ts`.

## Sources

- **Hecker, Raabe, Enslow, DeWeese, Maynard, van Prooijen 2008, "Real-time Motion Retargeting to Highly Varied User-Created Morphologies"**, ACM TOG 27(3) (SIGGRAPH 2008), https://chrishecker.com/Real-time_Motion_Retargeting_to_Highly_Varied_User-Created_Morphologies (paper PDF read: sections 1.2, 3, 4.2, 4.3). What it says:
  - "The player creates new characters in Spore by manipulating a malleable clay-like torso containing the spine, and attaching limbs and deformable anatomical body parts chosen from a palette". Characters "usually contain between 20 and 80 bodies"; bodies carry tags (*capabilities*: grasper, mouth, foot, spine) that tell the animation what they are.
  - Animations are authored once in a *generalized*, morphology-independent space (movement relative to the rest pose, to the ground, scaled by limb length) and *specialized* onto each creature at runtime, then handed to an IK solver.
  - Gaits: "A leg is defined as a path through the tree of connected limb segments with a foot body at the leaf and a spine segment at the base of the tree — called the hip." Legs are grouped by length ("harmonized by approximating their length ratios with small rational numbers"), and each foot gets a "locomotion duty factor and step trigger": "The duty factor is the fraction of the duration of the overall gait cycle time that the foot is on the ground … The step trigger is the offset from the overall gait cycle at which the foot begins its particular cycle." "The hips are translated and rotated as the feet are moved for believable torso motion."
  - "A heuristic is used to determine if a character with no feet should float above the ground or crawl along the ground. Crawling characters have some spine bodies converted into pseudo-feet and an inch-worm-like gait".
  - The IK is a particle solver (points with length constraints, after Jakobsen 2001), two phases: spine first, then limbs. The spine is fitted with splines (quintic Hermite, because "it is common for player created spines to have many inflection points"), and each spine body is stored as a parameter along the spline plus "the relative offset and relative orientation of the body with respect to the transported frame". That is how bodies ride along when the spine bends.
- **Spore's skin**: regenerated in real time as the player drags the spine, as a blobby implicit surface ("metaballs"), one metaball per spine point, the mouse wheel changing a point's density; implicit surfaces were chosen because they are topologically robust (Chris Hecker, "My Liner Notes for Spore", https://www.chrishecker.com/My_Liner_Notes_for_Spore, and the Godot forum summary https://forum.godotengine.org/t/metaballs-for-mesh-generation-spore-creature-creator/92992).
- **Spore's paint**: DeBry, Goffin, Hecker, Quigley, Shodhan et al., "Player-driven procedural texturing", SIGGRAPH 2007 talk, https://history.siggraph.org/learning/player-driven-procedural-texturing-by-debry-goffin-hecker-quigley-shodhan-et-al/: a middle way between "a full painting interface" and "simple compositions of preauthored textures". Jane Ng, "Spore – Rigblocks and SkinPaint", https://janeng.com/?p=143: parts (*rigblocks*) are hand-made, rigged meshes with named animation clips; the player's *skin paint* is blended into hand-authored textures through diffuse-alpha, specular and bump masks, with three tint layers.
- **Wilson 1966** (insect walking), as summarised in https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.05738 and https://demonstrations.wolfram.com/SimplifiedModelsOfTerrestrialArthropodGaits/: "A wave of protractions (swing movements of the legs) runs from posterior to anterior", "Contralateral legs of the same segment alternate in phase", and these rules give "a speed-dependent continuum" from wave gaits to the tripod.
- Gait numbers already in `docs/research/animals.md`: dynamic similarity (stride = 2.3·Fr^0.3·h), walking and trotting Froude numbers, and "Duty factors over 50% are considered a 'walk', while those less than 50% are considered a run."

## What the game had

The animal lab's animals (`gen/animalForm.ts`) are already built the Spore way: a spine with an ellipse of skin round each node, legs as jointed tubes, horns, ears, crests and eyes, all as data (`AnimalSkeleton`) that `surface/animalMesh.ts` turns into a smooth mesh, and a material (`surface/animalLook.ts`) that draws a countershaded coat with stripes, spots or patches. But the skeleton comes from a fixed template (three body plans and a few dozen numbers), and the walk is played in the vertex shader by swinging each leg about its hip, which `animals.md` notes is stylised: "the foot matches the ground's speed only on average … Hecker's duty factors and authored foot paths would fix it, at the cost of IK in the shader."

## The approach

**One body format, two ways in.** The editor makes the same `AnimalSkeleton` the generator does, so the game's mesh builder and material draw an edited creature unchanged. A `CreatureDesign` (`gen/creature.ts`) is what the player edits:

- **Spine**: 3 to 20 vertebrae in the body's middle plane, each with a radius (girth) and a width against height. A Catmull-Rom spline through them gives the skin's rings (4 per gap), so a dozen vertebrae make a smooth body. This stands in for Spore's metaballs: rings round a spline can't change topology, but here the spine is a single chain (no branches) and z only rises along it, so they never need to; the skin stays one tube domed at both ends.
- **Parts are anchored to the spine, not to space**: a part is `(s, θ)` on the skin, how far along the spine (0 the tail's tip, 1 the snout, a little beyond either end climbs the end's dome) and which way round it (0 on top, π/2 the left side, π underneath), plus size, a lean and a sprawl. Moving or fattening a vertebra moves everything on it, as Spore keeps each body's offset in the frame carried along its spine spline. Pairs are mirrored by θ → −θ. Picking a point on the body (`anchorOf`) finds the ring plane that holds it, then its angle; adding or removing a vertebra remaps every `s` so parts stay put.
- **Paint**: the coat the generator uses (back, belly, pattern and its colour and size, horns and claws, eyes) plus brush dabs. A dab is anchored to the spine like a part (an `s` and an offset in the spine's frame there), so strokes follow the body when it's reshaped. The material reads up to 800 dabs (400, each optionally mirrored) per pixel from a small float texture, so strokes are as sharp as the screen, not the mesh's vertices. The pattern and the dabs are laid out on the rest pose (an `aRestPos` attribute), so they move with the body instead of swimming over it as it walks.

**Motion that adapts to any body** (`gen/creatureMotion.ts`), the parts of Hecker's system a prototype needs:

- **Gait from leg placement.** Legs are ranked along the spine. A leg's phase is `rank × spacing`, plus half a stride for the right side, with `spacing` growing from `1/(2·ranks)` walking to ½ trotting. That one rule is Wilson's: for four legs it gives the lateral-sequence walk (LH 0, LF ¼, RH ½, RF ¾, the same phases `GAIT_PHASES` uses) and the diagonal trot; for six, the wave gait and alternating tripods; for any number, a wave from back to front. Unit tests check these cases.
- **Feet planted, not swung.** Each foot spends the duty factor (0.65 walking, 0.42 trotting, either side of the 50% line) on the ground sliding back under the body at exactly the body's speed, then swings forward on an arc. Speed and stride come from the game's own dynamic-similarity gait (`animalGait`) for the creature's mean hip height, so a long-legged creature strides and a stubby one patters.
- **Two-bone IK** from each hip (wherever the posed body puts it) to its foot, bending the knee towards a pole (forward for hind legs, back for the front pair, outward as the leg sprawls). Arms are the same, reaching for a hand target that swings against the stride.
- **Spine**: a travelling sideways wave (big and fast without legs, a slither; small with legs), a bob twice a stride, breathing. Legless creatures are lowered onto the floor.

All of this runs on the CPU every frame and rebuilds the mesh in place (topology doesn't change with the pose, a unit test checks it). It costs about 7 ms a frame for one creature in the headless test browser, fine for an editor. Herds in the game would need it cheaper (below).

## The prototype (creature.html)

- **Build**: drag the green spine dots in the middle plane; the wheel over one fattens it (Shift widens it); pulling an end dot out grows the spine. Click a part in the palette and it follows the pointer over the skin as a live preview until a click sticks it on (Shift keeps placing), or drag it from the palette. Drag a part's yellow dot to slide it over the body, the wheel to resize. Legs and arms have three nodes: the yellow one where they sit on the body, and pink ones for the knee or elbow and the foot or hand. Knees, elbows and hands are dragged in the view's plane, feet slide over the ground. They are kept as offsets from the limb's root (mirrored for the other side), the walk's two-bone IK keeps each joint bending the way it was posed, and a leg whose body has since been raised grows to reach its foot. A mouth sits anywhere on the skin like an eye (the snout by default): a dark opening between two lip tubes, curved from frown to smile, opened by a slider and in a yawn now and then, with optional teeth. It is built with the same number of triangles whether shut or open, so the posed mesh still updates in place. The inspector edits the selection (girth and width; size, lean, sprawl, mirroring), with undo and redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z) and Delete.
- **Paint**: the coat's colours and pattern, a random coat, and a brush (colour, size, hardness, mirror) that paints on the body.
- **Play**: the creature walks or trots on a moving floor, with a live footfall diagram (a row per leg, filled while its foot is down).
- New creatures: the starting blob, a random creature (`randomCreature`: 0 to 4 pairs of legs, arms, 0 to 3 pairs of eyes or a cyclops, a mouth, horns, ears, antennae, spikes), or one of the game's own generated animals turned into a design (`designFromAnimal`). The page's `#hash` holds the design, so a link shares it.

## Next steps

- **Into the game**: an edited creature is already an `AnimalSkeleton`; a species could carry a design instead of a form, and `SurfaceAnimals` would draw its herds. The CPU IK is too dear for herds: the planet would keep the shader's swinging legs (with each leg's phase from `legPhase`) for distant animals and IK only the nearest few.
- **Skin**: rings can't do branching bodies (two heads, a forked tail). Spore's metaball skin, polygonised with marching cubes or surface nets, would allow them, at the cost of rebuilding a larger mesh on each edit.
- **Parts as meshes**: hands, feet and wings (mouths are procedural for now) as small authored meshes with named deformations (Spore's rigblocks), and tags (capabilities) that the animation reads, e.g. a mouth for eating, graspers for picking fruit.
- **Gaits**: legs of very different lengths should be grouped and harmonised (Hecker's rational ratios) rather than sharing one stride; uneven ground needs the feet raycast to the terrain.
- **Paint**: brush dabs anchored to limbs (they're anchored to the spine, so a dab on a swinging leg stays where the leg stood); more patterns and Spore-style paint layers.
