# Three.js game conventions

Browser game: Vite + TypeScript (strict), `three`, `@dimforge/rapier3d-compat` physics, `lil-gui` + `stats.js` debug tools, Vitest tests. The theme is Spore's **space stage**: a UFO flying around star systems.

## Architecture map

| Path | Role |
|---|---|
| `src/main.ts` | Composition root: read `?seed` / `?star`, generate the galaxy, init Rapier and debug, create `Game` and the `SceneManager`, start. Installs the console log for the debug dump first. Exposes `window.game` / `galaxy` / `levels` (the `SceneManager`), `debugDump` and live getters `ship` / `world` (the `StarSystem` entity) / `system` (its data) for the current system level in dev. |
| `src/core/Game.ts` | Renderer, shared camera, input, resize, main loop. Steps and renders only the active `Level` (`game.setLevel`), after a few global entities (`game.add`, e.g. the `SceneManager`). During a crossfade (`game.setCrossfade(from, weight)`) the outgoing level is stepped, updated and drawn too, and `Crossfade.ts` mixes the two final pictures (copy of the canvas + an overlay quad). `paused` freezes time (no fixed steps, zero frame time) but keeps drawing (the menu sets it). `afterFrame`, `crossfadeSolo` and `redraw()` are automation hooks (the screenshot tool uses them). `FIXED_DT = 1/60`. |
| `src/levels/` | `Level` (own `THREE.Scene`, entity list, optional `Physics`, `enter`/`exit`, overridable `render`), `SystemLevel`, `GalaxyLevel`, `PlanetLevel`, and `SceneManager` (owns the levels and runs the transitions, blocking input meanwhile). Every transition (galaxy ↔ system, system ↔ planet) is a seamless zoom: one pure timeline (`seamlessZoom.ts`) drives both levels' cameras; the outgoing level plays alone, then both are drawn and crossfaded framed identically (mapped through `galacticTilt` or the planet's body frame), then the incoming one. Arriving from the galaxy, the ship flies in along the ecliptic to hover above the star, and coming back up from a planet it hovers above that, with the camera zoomed out and looking down far enough to keep the body in view (`arrival.ts`: `arrivalParams`, `leaveParams`, `clampElevation`, `hoverViewElevation`). Arriving at a star the camera sits low and turns to look at the star itself, the ship hovering above it in view (`OrbitCamera.setAim`, kept by `SystemLevel.aimAt` until the ship goes to another body; it stops short of losing the ship from the view). |
| `src/core/Entity.ts` | The only lifecycle contract (see below). |
| `src/core/FixedStep.ts` | Pure fixed-timestep accumulator. |
| `src/core/Input.ts` | Polled keys (`KeyboardEvent.code`), pointer position, click-vs-drag, wheel; touch turned into the same gestures by pure `touch.ts` (`TouchGestures`, `stickAxes`); analog key presses from on-screen controls (`setAnalog`); `touchMode`. |
| `src/core/Debug.ts` | `debug.folder(name)` → lil-gui folder or `undefined` in prod. Re-requesting a name replaces the folder (levels get rebuilt), so give each level's folders distinct names (`System camera`, `Galaxy camera`). |
| `src/physics/Physics.ts` | Rapier world (zero gravity). Re-exports `RAPIER`. |
| `src/gen/` | **Procedural generation, pure data, no THREE/DOM/Rapier**: `rng.ts` (seeded PRNG, `hashSeed`), `galaxy.ts`, `stars.ts`, `system.ts`, `planets.ts`, `climate.ts` (temperature, atmosphere, internal heat; split into a fixed setting, a terraformable state and derived values that `evaluateClimate` recomputes, so change a climate with `terraform(climate, change)`, never by editing derived fields), `atmosphere.ts` (an atmosphere's look from its climate: stylised scale height, shell top and haze depth, plus the optical-depth integral the atmosphere shader mirrors), `belts.ts` (step 26: a system's asteroid belts from the gaps its planets leave: a main belt between the last rocky planet and the first giant at the real resonance edges' log fractions, with Kirkwood gaps; an icy outer belt from the outermost planet's 3:2; Trojan swarms at a giant's L4/L5; named asteroids with lazily built shapes (`asteroidBody`); the scenery rocks (`generateRocks`) and `rockPosition`, which the view's shader mirrors), `comets.ts` (comets on Kepler orbits, each with a visitable nucleus from its own stream: seed, spin, dark colours and a `ShapeData`; `cometActivity` (the tails' 1/r², off past 3 habitable radii), `cometVents`), `shape.ts` (step 25: irregular small bodies, **one shape function for every lumpy body**: `shapeRadius(shape, dir)`, a smooth union of 1–3 ellipsoid lobes that all contain the centre (so the surface is one radius per direction and the cube sphere, LOD, ground ray and map keep working), plus lumps and craters, normalised to a longest reach of 1 and never below `SHAPE_FLOOR`; contact binaries keep a neck of at least `MIN_NECK`; `shapeNormal`, `shapeExtents`; step 26's asteroids build on it), `starActivity.ts` (per-kind star behaviour and storm events on a seeded time-slot grid), `gasGiants.ts` (gas and ice giants' cloud tops as data from their own stream: belts and zones, Jupiter-like to Saturn-like, the jets at their edges as a share of the spin (`gasDrift`), the polar region with its cyclones or a polygonal jet, storms (a great spot, white ovals; ice giants' dark spot with its bright companion, methane streaks, a polar hood); see docs/research/gas-giants.md), `lavaActivity.ts` (a lava body's vents, fountain and eruption events on the same kind of slot grid, cached by `EruptionSchedule`, and the ballistic arc maths), `geysers.ts` (which bodies have cryo / steam / sulphur geysers from their climate, the vents on the ground, each vent's eruption cycles cached by `GeyserSchedule`, and plume motion under drag, gravity or buoyancy and wind), `weather.ts` (which bodies have water, acid, methane or dust weather from their climate, and volcanic lightning; cloud cover, storms on a seeded slot grid cached by `StormSchedule`, the ash clouds of big eruptions, lightning flashes on a finer grid, allocation-free), `cloudNoise.ts` (the clouds' tileable, equalised 3D noise), `nebulas.ts` (the galaxy's named nebulas: emission, reflection, dark, planetary and supernova remnants round fitting stars, from their own stream; their smooth density model, `nebulaAt` for which one a point is in (`StarRef.nebula`, `SystemData.nebula`), and the dark ones' star dimming in closed form), `rogues.ts` (step 28: the galaxy's rogue planets, `GalaxyData.rogues`, from their own stream: `StarRef`s with no stars whose ids follow on from the stars' (`systemRef(galaxy, id)` finds either, so `?star=` and travel work alike; `isRogue`); `generateSystem` turns one into a starless "system" of one still planet at the centre (orbit radius 0, `habitableRadius` 0) and maybe a moon, warmed only from inside: airless at (F/σ)^¼ ≈ 35 K or under a thick `hydrogen` atmosphere (the climate's only composition with a gravity term, τ ∝ (P/√g)^1.161), and an ice world whose sea comes out liquid is an ocean world; `rogueClimate`), `plants.ts` (a habitable body's plant species from its seed and tier (each with a `PlantForm`; tree crowns sized by architecture, `TREE_CROWN_WIDTH`), and where they stand: cells of the cube-sphere grid whose plants depend only on the planet's seed and the cell's address, rejected by beach, tree line, slope, latitude temperature and a fertility field; stable plant ids; the latitude temperature model), `plantForm.ts` (plant lab: how a species grows, `growPlant` → a `PlantSkeleton` of stems (polylines with radii, thinning by Leonardo's rule, turning by the golden angle) and leaf masses (ellipsoids) or a palm's fronds, for four architectures (conifer, broadleaf, palm, shrub), stretched to fill the species' envelope exactly; `generateForm`), `names.ts`, `color.ts`, plus shared math (`orbit.ts`: circular and Kepler orbits, `noise.ts`, `quat.ts`: plain-data quaternions, `galactic.ts`: a system's `galacticTilt`, kept within 30° of the galactic plane by `flatTilt` → the galaxy band as seen from it). |
| `src/world/` | Views that render generated data: `StarSystem` (from `SystemData`; owns the system clock: `time`, `setTime`, `pose`), `Star` (animated surface and corona in `StarLook.ts` / `starMaterials.ts`, storms in `StarStorms.ts`, all driven by `animate(time)`; the surface is drawn at a fixed over-bright `starParams.intensity`, bleeding into white, and the corona billboard is a bright inner rim with streamers plus a smooth glare falling off as r^-2.6 in star radii, no eye adaptation), `Planet` (also moons and comet nuclei: `PlanetConfig.shape` makes it an irregular small body, `path` a Kepler orbit, `small: 'comet'` gives it jets and a coma in low orbit), `Comet` (a comet's coma sprite and tails in the system view, following its nucleus, a `Planet` in `StarSystem.nuclei` and `bodies`; the tails are billboarded ribbons that fade out seen end-on, where a glow round the head takes over; `cometConfig` turns its data into the nucleus's config), `AsteroidBelt` (a belt's rocks as dots in one `Points` and, near the camera, tumbling instanced meshes chosen on the CPU, all moved in the vertex shader from the clock, at most `beltParams.maxMeshes` per belt (over it, the size a rock needs for a mesh rises; batches sorted by orbit radius and whole far belts skipped, `rockBounds` / `rocksOutOfReach`, so choosing is cheap while the camera moves), plus a faint dusty ring whose pattern (edges, gaps, clumps, a Trojan swarm's arc, which is all its mesh covers) is baked once into a texture by `beltDust.ts`; a `Region` the `Picker` hovers by its own ray test, a click flying to the nearest named asteroid; the named asteroids are `Planet`s in `StarSystem.asteroids` and `bodies`, made by `asteroidConfig`), `planetGeometry.ts` (terrain, gas bands and rings, shared with the planet level, all on cube spheres from `cubeSphere.ts` (no poles, indexed); `terrainSampler` / `gasSampler` give the surface's radius and colour per direction; `terrainPainter` / `gasPainter` colour a surface point, so anything showing the surface, like the map, matches the globe), `atmosphereShell.ts` (the soft atmosphere shader for both views: integrates the haze along each view ray, works from outside and inside; atmospheres draw after the opaque scene and the UFO after them, via `ATMOSPHERE_RENDER_ORDER`), `SkyStars` (the galaxy's other stars in a system's sky, each where it really is and as bright as luminosity / distance², squeezed to flux^0.4, from pure `gen/galactic.ts` `skyStars`; the old random `Starfield` is only the lab's sky now), `GalaxyBand` (the galaxy's band in the sky, baked once into a cube map and drawn at infinity with a rotation-only view), `OrbitTrails` (grey smoky ribbons behind each body along its orbit), `noiseGlsl.ts` (shared GLSL value noise and simplex noise), `lavaMaterial.ts` (`LavaLook`: the animated lava sea, per pixel for the planet level's sea sphere and, in the system view and on local moons, per vertex painted onto the terrain's own flat sea (`paintTerrain`: one surface, so nothing z-fights from afar), plus the vent glow; a pure function of the system clock via `animate(time)`, which `StarSystem` calls for every body), `gasLook.ts` (`GasLook`: a giant's clouds drawn per pixel onto its lit material (`apply`) from a baked latitude table and the shared 3D noise, the same GLSL (`GAS_GLSL`) on the system view's sphere, the low-orbit globe and the planet map: drifting bands, churning that bends the band edges at the jets, storms that twist the clouds round them, polar cyclones, finer octaves as the camera nears; a pure function of the clock via `animate`; `gasParams`), `weatherLook.ts` (`WeatherLook`: a body's cloud layer for both views, with its storms and lightning flashes as uniforms, a pure function of the clock via `animate`; `weatherParams.enabled` is the menu's Weather setting; clouds, rain and bolts draw after the atmosphere, `CLOUD_RENDER_ORDER`), `nebulaLook.ts` (a nebula as a ray-marched volume for both the galaxy map and a system's sky, blended as light added plus what's behind times its transmittance; `NebulaSkyBake` bakes the nebulas round a system into a cube map once, and `STAR_DIMMING_GLSL` dims the map's stars behind dark nebulas), `galacticLight.ts` (a starless system's light: `StarSystem.galacticLight`, a dim directional light from the galactic centre, the same in low orbit (`PlanetLights`), with the air lit at `air` strength (`AtmosphereSun.strength`) and the levels' tone mapping opened up to `exposure`; `StarSystem.anchor` is the star, or the rogue planet, the ship arrives at; check `SystemLevel.starless` wherever a system's star is assumed), `NebulaSky` (that baked sky, drawn over the galaxy band, in two bakes: big or surrounding nebulas at 256², small ones in their own bake sized to the smallest (`detailBakeSize`, up to 1024²) so filaments stay sharp, composited small-behind-big; the band's stars and `SkyStars` are dimmed on the CPU instead), `noiseTexture.ts` (the shared 3D noise texture from `gen/cloudNoise.ts`). Stars/planets/moons/comet nuclei implement `CelestialBody` (a `Sight`, i.e. name, description, radius (a small body's longest reach) and render position, an optional `pickRadius` (a comet's coma), plus standoff, sim position and velocity); things you could hover but not fly to would implement just `Sight`; their positions are pure functions of the clock (`positionAt`). |

New feature areas get their own folder under `src/` (e.g. `src/combat/`, `src/audio/`).

## Procedural generation (`src/gen/`)

- **Data, then view.** Generators return plain serialisable data (`GalaxyData`, `StarRef`, `SystemData`, `PlanetData`, ...) with colours as hex strings. Classes in `src/world/` turn that data into meshes and bodies. Never put THREE objects in generated data.
- **Only `Rng`, never `Math.random`**, anywhere generated or visual content is decided (the starfield is seeded too). The same seed must give the same universe.
- **Fork per thing:** `rng.fork('planet', i)` gives an independent stream per object. Adding a draw to one planet must not change the next planet or another system. Derive seeds with `hashSeed(...)`.
- **Lazy by level:** `generateGalaxy(seed)` makes only `StarRef`s (position, name, star types, seed). `generateSystem(ref)` is called on demand and is fully determined by `ref`. Planet-surface detail should follow the same pattern from `PlanetData.seed`.
- **Scales:** each level has its own units. Galaxy units (`GALAXY_RADIUS = 1000`), system units (G star r≈30, UFO ≈4 wide) and planet units (the system view magnified by `PLANET_SCALE` = `EARTH_GLOBE_RADIUS` / 8 = 50 for every body, so an Earth-sized one has radius 400 and a dwarf 100, next to the same 4-unit UFO; centred in the body's own tilted, spinning frame. `EARTH_GLOBE_RADIUS` in `gen/planets.ts` is the one knob: the terrain's finest octaves, the LOD depth, the camera's zoom-out range, the autopilot's speed and the lava and geyser sizes follow it through `GLOBE_SIZE_FACTOR`) are unrelated. Convert at transitions (`planet/frame.ts` for planet ↔ system; `SystemData.galacticTilt` plus the galaxy root's turn for system ↔ galaxy) and never mix them in one scene.
- **Real-world rules and numbers** (temperatures, atmospheres, gravity, star properties) come from the `research` skill: looked up, checked against real reference cases, recorded in `docs/research/`, and cited next to the constant. No guessed magic numbers.
- **Layout invariants** are covered by `tests/universe.test.ts` (no overlapping orbits or moons, a clear spawn point, Kepler-ordered periods). Extend it when adding generated features. When tuning, print sample systems from a throwaway test rather than guessing.
- Terrain: `terrainNoise` returns roughly [-1, 1] with a median of ~0. `PlanetStyle.seaLevel` is a threshold in that range (0 ≈ half the surface underwater).

## Entity lifecycle

Everything in the game implements `Entity` and is registered with its level (`level.add(entity)`, `level.remove(entity)` to take one out and dispose it at the end of the current step or update; only level-independent things like the `SceneManager` use `game.add`). Only the active level runs; inactive levels keep their state, frozen. The loop runs:

```
per fixed step (60 Hz):  fixedUpdate(dt) → physics.step() → afterPhysics()
per frame:               update(frameDt, alpha) → render
game.remove(e) / game.dispose()  → dispose()
```

- **`fixedUpdate`**: gameplay, AI, forces and impulses, kinematic targets. Anything that must behave the same at 30 or 144 FPS.
- **`afterPhysics`**: copy body transforms into `prev`/`curr` state.
- **`update`**: visuals only. Interpolate `mesh.position.lerpVectors(prev, curr, alpha)` and `quaternion.slerpQuaternions(...)`, plus cosmetic animation, cameras and HUD. Never apply forces here.
- **`dispose`**: remove from the scene, `.dispose()` every geometry, material and texture you created, `world.removeRigidBody(body)`, and remove DOM/event listeners.

Composite entities (e.g. `StarSystem` owning `Planet`s) forward the hooks to their children. Only the parent is registered with the level. A level disposes its entities and physics world in `dispose()`; anything added straight to its scene (e.g. a light) is disposed by a `dispose` override.

Constructor pattern: take the dependencies you need (`scene`, `physics`, `input`, `debug`, ...) explicitly. There is no global singleton. Each level's constructor wires its entities; `main.ts` wires the game.

## Physics rules (Rapier)

- Rapier is WASM. `main.ts` awaits `Physics.init()` once; after that `Physics.create(FIXED_DT)` makes a world synchronously (one per level that needs physics).
- Import it as `import { RAPIER, type Physics } from '../physics/Physics'`.
- Body types: **dynamic** for things pushed by forces (ship), **kinematicPositionBased** for scripted movers (orbiting planets, via `setNextKinematicTranslation` in `fixedUpdate`), **fixed** for static objects (sun).
- The ship locks rotations and sets its orientation directly from input. Movement uses `applyImpulse(force * dt)` so it scales with the fixed step. Forces added with `addForce` persist until `resetForces`, so prefer impulses.
- Rapier returns plain `{x,y,z}` objects and accepts any `{x,y,z}`/`{x,y,z,w}`, so THREE vectors and quaternions can be passed directly.
- Enable CCD on fast bodies (`setCcdEnabled(true)`).
- Never step the world anywhere except the fixed-step loop (`Game` → `level.fixedStep`).

## Rendering and performance rules

- **No per-frame allocation** in `fixedUpdate` / `update`: keep scratch `Vector3`/`Quaternion`/`Euler` objects as private readonly fields (see `Ship.ts`).
- Many identical objects (asteroids, stars, fleets) → `InstancedMesh` or `Points`, never thousands of meshes.
- Low-poly, flat-shaded, vertex-coloured style (`flatShading: true`, `vertexColors: true`). Procedural generation must be **seeded and deterministic** (`terrainNoise(x, y, z, seed)`).
- The renderer uses ACES tone mapping. Emissive or glowing objects use `MeshBasicMaterial` or additive sprites.
- Scale: the UFO is ~4 units wide, and distances are compressed (sun r=30, planets orbit at 90–330). The camera far plane is 20000 and the starfield sits at 9000. Galaxy scale lives in its own level (`GalaxyLevel`), never in huge system coordinates (float precision).
- Pixel ratio is capped at 2.
- Animated visuals that must look the same wherever the clock is (living stars, comets) are **pure functions of the system time**, set from `StarSystem.update` (interpolated time) and `pose` (the planet level's sky). Random events use a seeded slot grid (`gen/starActivity.ts`) instead of a running `Rng`, so any moment can be shown without replaying the past.
- Shader-animated particles: one pooled `Points` with the motion in the vertex shader; write new particles into a ring buffer and upload only those ranges (`addUpdateRange`), see `StarStorms`. Keep per-pixel noise off large billboards (compute it per vertex).

## Tunables and debug

Gameplay constants live in an exported `xxxParams` object next to the code that uses them (`shipParams`, `cameraParams`) and are bound in the constructor:

```ts
const f = debug.folder('Ship');
f?.add(shipParams, 'thrust', 0, 300);
f?.add(shipParams, 'linearDamping', 0, 5).onChange((v: number) => body.setLinearDamping(v));
```

Debug is on in `npm run dev` and in any build with `?debug` in the URL. lil-gui and stats.js are dynamically imported, so they cost nothing in production.

## Input

Use `input.isDown('KeyW')` / `input.axis('KeyA', 'KeyD')` (codes, not `key`). Mouse: `input.pointer` (NDC + client coords, live object), `consumeClick()` (left press that moved < 5 px), `consumeDrag()` (left/right drag pixels), `consumeWheel()` (pixels, + = zoom out). No pointer lock. Avoid Ctrl-combos (Ctrl+W closes the tab).

Touch (phones) goes through the same calls, so game code never checks for it: a tap is a click, one-finger drag is a drag, a pinch is the wheel (spreading the fingers ×2 halves the view distance), and a finger held still is the hovering pointer (tooltips). The on-screen stick and hold buttons (`TouchControls`) press keys as analog values (`input.setAnalog('KeyW', 0.6)`), so `axis` can return fractions: normalise a move vector only when its length is over 1. Every new control needs a touch path: a key-only action gets a hold button in `#touch-controls` (and `Level.touchControls` says which levels show it), and help text gets touch wording (`HelpText`). The canvas has `touch-action: none`; overlays that shouldn't eat touches keep `pointer-events: none`. Audio unlocks on a touch's *release* (browsers don't count a touch `pointerdown` as a gesture).

Current controls (Spore-style): in a system **there's no manual flying**: the ship always hovers above a body (straight above it, on the ecliptic's north side, the hull drifting gently) or is on the autopilot to the next one. Left-click a star/planet/moon (in the view or on the system map) to fly there; clicking empty space does nothing; N folds the map away. Scroll zooms and **moves the ship with it**: at a body, the camera distance sets how high above it the ship hovers; over a planet it sets the altitude (levels read `orbit.zoom` each frame unless `Level.zoomLocked`, which transitions set). Drag rotates the camera. Shift boosts the autopilot. Scrolling in past min zoom while hovering at a planet or moon (or flying into one) descends to its low orbit; coming back up, the ship reappears above it and the camera zooms out to take in the planet and its moons. In low orbit WASD still nudges the planet ship. While the autopilot (or a galaxy jump) is under way, the camera zooms freely (the ship's hover height follows it) but scrolling past a limit changes no level (`zoomLimitsHold` in `OrbitCamera`): you descend, leave for the galaxy or enter a system only once the ship has arrived, and the camera never ends up inside a body (`keepOut`, which the system level uses to push it out of every body); there, clicking the globe (the ray meets the terrain as drawn, so a hillside click flies to that hillside) or the map flies the great circle to that point, and zoomed in low the UFO keeps a fixed clearance over the ground beneath it (rising before slopes), N folds the map away, and scrolling out past max returns to the system. On touch: tap for click, pinch for scroll, drag to rotate, hold a finger on something to identify it, the stick for WASD (low orbit only), and Boost and Map (opens the system or planet map overlay) buttons.

Visual-only entities that read the camera (`Picker`, `TargetMarker`, `Hud`) are added after `OrbitCamera`, so they see this frame's camera. Billboards should `lookAt(camera.position)` rather than copy the camera quaternion (or use a `Sprite`): facing the view plane makes them poke through spheres when off-centre.

## Testing

- Put pure logic (math, generation, rules, state machines) in modules with **no THREE scene / DOM / Rapier dependency** and unit-test them in `tests/*.test.ts`.
- Rendering and feel are verified in the browser with the `run-game` skill.

## Checklist for every feature

1. Code goes in the right folder and implements `Entity` if it lives in the loop, with a complete `dispose()`.
2. Simulation in `fixedUpdate`, visuals interpolated in `update`, no per-frame allocations.
3. New tunables are exposed through `debug.folder`.
4. `npm run typecheck` and `npm test` pass (add tests for new pure logic).
5. `npm run build` succeeds.
6. Verify in the browser with the `run-game` skill: renders, no console errors, FPS is stable.
